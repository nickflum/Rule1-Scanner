How this works
  • Data: annual financials from SEC EDGAR filings (10-K), daily prices from Yahoo Finance (Stooq as backup), macro series from FRED. Refreshed each weekday after the US close.
  • Companies: every company listed on the NYSE, Nasdaq or Cboe (plus the S&P 500) that files annual reports with the SEC. Blank-cheque companies (SPACs), funds, companies without a recent 10-K, and foreign companies reporting in another currency are left out. Over-the-counter stocks aren't included. Market cap = today's price × diluted shares from the latest annual report.
  • Industries: the SEC's industry classification (SIC) for every company, so the picker uses one consistent list. Sectors are the S&P (GICS) sector where known, otherwise the closest match.
  • Big Five score (0–100): for ROIC, sales, EPS, equity (book value per share where possible) and free cash flow, the 10-, 5- and 1-year rates are scored: ≥10% scores full marks, 5–10% half. Weights 40/40/20 for 10y/5y/1y, averaged across the five. A green dot means that metric cleared 10% over both 10 and 5 years.
  • ROIC = after-tax operating income ÷ (equity + long-term debt). Free cash flow = operating cash flow − capital expenditure.
  • Debt test: years of current free cash flow needed to repay long-term debt. Town wants 3 or fewer.
  • Sticker Price: latest EPS grown at the chosen growth rate for 10 years × future P/E (2 × growth rate, capped), discounted back at your MARR. MOS price = Sticker Price × (1 − margin of safety). Free data has no analyst forecasts, so historical growth is used and capped.
  • Priority score: 60% Big Five quality + 40% valuation (100 at or below the MOS price, falling to 0 at the Sticker Price), minus 10 if debt takes more than 3 years to repay. Only meant to order your reading list.
  • Tools: Town's timing signals — price above its 10-day average, MACD (8,17,9) above its signal line, stochastic (14,5) %K above %D. Green = "buy-side". These are short-term and noisy.
  • Watchlist: click ☆ next to any company to add it, ★ to remove it. Stars are saved in this browser, so your phone and computer each keep their own list. Tickers in watchlist.txt on GitHub start out starred everywhere.
  • Limits: XBRL data can be patchy (especially banks, insurers and REITs, whose statements don't fit the Big Five well). Always confirm numbers in the actual filings before acting. This is a research shortlist, not advice.
